    Why compress locally

    Photos carry more than pixels. A picture straight off a phone usually contains EXIF metadata — the exact GPS coordinates where it was taken, the device, the timestamp. Uploading a batch of holiday or property photos to a compression site hands all of that to a third party. Compressing in the browser keeps the file on your machine, and because the image is redrawn onto a canvas the location metadata is dropped from the output as a side effect.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

    No. The conversion runs in your browser. Load the page, go offline, and it still works.

    What quality setting should I use?

    70–80% is usually indistinguishable from the original while roughly halving the file size. For print, stay above 90%.

    I chose PNG and the file got bigger.

    PNG is lossless, which is inefficient for photographs. For photos, choose JPG or WebP instead.

    My transparent background turned white.

    JPG does not support transparency, so it is filled with white. Choose PNG or WebP to keep it.

    How many images can I do at once?

    There is no fixed limit, but since this uses your device's memory, batches of a few dozen at a time are more comfortable.
