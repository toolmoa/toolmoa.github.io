Why these tools are different

Your file never reaches a server

Almost every "online converter" works the same way: you upload your file, their server processes it, and you download the result. Your document sits on someone else's machine, at least temporarily. These tools do the work in JavaScript inside your own browser. Nothing is transmitted — you can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and every tool still works.

That matters for work documents

Contracts, invoices, ID scans, internal reports — plenty of things you might want to merge or compress are things you should not be uploading to an unknown service. Here the question does not arise.

No account, no watermark, no daily limit

There is no email to hand over, no logo stamped on your output and no "3 files per day" cap. Since there is no server doing the work, there is nothing to meter.

Frequently asked questions

Is this really free?

Yes, with no limits. Because the processing happens on your own device rather than on a server, running these tools costs us nothing per use.

How can I verify my files are not uploaded?

Load the page, then disconnect from the internet or switch on airplane mode. Every tool keeps working. You can also open your browser's developer tools and watch the Network tab — no request is made when you process a file.

Is there a file size limit?

There is no service-imposed limit. The practical limit is your device's memory, so very large files may be slow on an older phone.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. Everything works the same in a mobile browser and there is no app to install.
