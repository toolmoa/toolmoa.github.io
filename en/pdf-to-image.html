Why convert a PDF locally

Plenty of places still will not take a PDF: chat apps, social media, most blog editors, slides where you want one page as a picture, a signed page you need to send as an image. The documents involved — contracts, statements, internal reports — are the last things you want sitting on a stranger's conversion server. This page opens the PDF inside your browser with Mozilla's pdf.js, draws each page onto a canvas and saves that. The file never leaves your machine, and the page keeps working offline.

Common uses

Posting online — PDFs cannot be posted most places, so post the pages as JPG.
Inserting into documents — as a picture in Word or PowerPoint, the page looks right even without the fonts.
Thumbnails — save just the first page as a cover image for a listing.
Printing — save at 3× so text stays sharp on paper.

Frequently asked questions

Is my PDF uploaded anywhere?

No. It is processed inside your browser and nothing is sent anywhere. The page keeps working with the internet switched off, so it is safe for contracts and work documents.

JPG or PNG?

For pages that are mostly photos, JPG is far smaller. For tables, drawings and small text, PNG is sharper — but it can be 3–10× the size of JPG for the same page. If unsure, try JPG first and switch to PNG only if the text looks smudged.

Which resolution should I choose?

For viewing on screen or sending in a chat, 2× is plenty. For printing or zooming in, go to 3× or above. Higher resolution means sharper text but larger files and longer conversion. The exact pixel size is shown under the setting as you change it.

Is there a page limit?

No fixed limit. Since all the work happens on your device, many pages at a high resolution take time and memory — selecting only the pages you need is faster than saving hundreds at 4×. If a single page would be too large, it is scaled down automatically and you are told.

The text looks blurry.

Raise the resolution and switch to PNG. JPG leaves faint smudges around sharp edges, which shows on tables and small print. Setting JPG quality to 100% also helps.

Does it work with password-protected PDFs?

Encrypted PDFs cannot be opened. Remove the password first, then try again.
